Do their numbers match?

Checking Indonesian statistical claims against official BPS data, and what goes wrong when they don't.

What I found

Officials citing BPS almost always got the number right. The two mismatches came from news copy: a stale figure (unemployment quoted as 4.65% nine days after BPS published 4.75%) and a typo.

The automated checkers failed elsewhere. They misread which statistic or which period a sentence meant, accepted out-of-date numbers because they assume an old BPS release calendar, and leaked numbers between sentences batched in one prompt. On ambiguous sentences no model matched a human decision on more than 2 of 7.
